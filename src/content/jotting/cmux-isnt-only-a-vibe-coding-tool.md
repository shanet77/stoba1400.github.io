It's a bit of an overreaction, I know. 
<br>
When cmux first released, my feeds were inundated with posts highlighting how they could manage all of their AI coding sessions in a single place. Raving about the productivity gains.
<br>
This immediately made me skeptical[^1]. It was based on [libghostty](https://github.com/ghostty-org/ghostty), so why would I not just use [ghostty](https://ghostty.org/download)? And to boot, that vertical workspace manager took up screen real estate. 
<br>
3 months later, an _eternity_ in this space, I decided to give it a try. 
<br>
I gotta say, it's pretty nice!
<br>
The perks of libghostty are present and I was wrong about the vertical workspace tab, it's handy being able to create workspaces based on the projects I'm working on and easily switch between them.
<br>
Notably, I find it much easier to _identify_ which project belongs to each workspace. I had a much harder time with this while using the ghostty horizontal tabs. I am fully open to the idea that I'm not using ghostty to it's full capacity. A [skill issue](https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=skill+issue), one may say. Regardless, cmux fits well into my workflow. Typically at home, I'm splitting time between this site, [HoopNerd](https://hoopnerd.xyz/)[^2], and my home server. It's nice labelling them[^3]
<br>
Quick note, I am **not** a fan of the built-in browser. It's a neat idea, but I like having my browser tabs in the standalone browser. Especially when working in professional environments, where sites may not work properly in the cmux browser. This is to say that `openTerminalLinksInCmuxBrowser` was immediately disabled.
<br>
Also needed to change the keymapping for `globalSearch` as that was conflicting with my [Rectangle](https://rectangleapp.com/)[^4] keymaps. Other than that, it was a pleasant plug-and-play experience, which is what I want from my terminal emulator. So, uh, give it a shot!


[^1]: It's a bad habit, but I can tend to be a contrarian. Especially in the AI space.
[^2]: Try it out! I can't imagine there's consistent enough traffic for p2p matches, but CPU is always available!
[^3]: With great names like `HomeServ` and `Site`.
